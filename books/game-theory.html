About this book

Game theory asks what rational players will do when each one’s outcome depends on the others. Today many of the players are algorithms — pricing engines, bidding agents, poker and Go programs, trading systems — and the question is no longer only which equilibrium, but whether learning players reach one, how fast, and what they do instead.

This book covers the major types of games, their players, outcomes and Nash equilibria, and the AI methods that now play them better than people do. It then introduces the Veeraghanta space: games whose rules change with the play — in the limit, a dynamic change in the rules with every move — and applies it to banks, traders and hedge funds facing regulators, dealers, clearing houses and prime brokers that rewrite the rules in response.
