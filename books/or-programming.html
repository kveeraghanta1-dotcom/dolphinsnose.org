About this book

Linear, nonlinear, integer and mixed-integer programs all spend their time in linear algebra. Interior-point methods, Karmarkar’s legacy, factor a new matrix at every step and, as this book proves, cannot keep the Kronecker structure a problem has. The Kronecker–Veeraghanta algorithm keeps its operator fixed: it decomposes the coupling once with Kronecker algebra and pushes every bound, penalty, nonconvexity and integrality condition into a separable step.

The same decomposition then serves every iteration, restart and branch-and-bound node. Each problem class gets its own theory, its own case studies against the simplex method, Karmarkar’s algorithm, interior point and branch-and-cut, and an honest account of where the algorithm loses.
