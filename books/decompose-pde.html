About this book

Every large nonlinear program eventually reduces to a linear system, and Gaussian elimination makes that step cost cubic time and quadratic memory. This book rebuilds the theory of nonlinear programming around a different idea: write the dominant operator as a Kronecker sum, diagonalize it exactly with a fast sine transform, and eliminate state and adjoint variables analytically.

The optimality conditions, multipliers and duality stay classical. The computation does not: an exact direct solver for separable operators in O(N log N) work and O(N) memory, mesh-independent preconditioning, reduced-space elimination, and inexact Newton–Krylov methods with q-superlinear local convergence, compared against dense and sparse KKT solvers with every proof given two ways.
